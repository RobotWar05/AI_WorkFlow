# Evidence Research

Produce a decision-ready answer, not a pile of links.

## Workflow

1. Restate the decision or question being researched.
2. Split it into claims that can be independently verified.
3. Inspect user-provided and local sources first.
4. Search current sources when a claim is time-sensitive or uncertain.
5. Prefer primary sources: official documentation, specifications, repositories, release notes, papers, and first-party announcements.
6. Use independent sources for adoption, operational experience, or disputed claims.
7. Record publication date, event/version date, author or owner, and scope.
8. Resolve conflicts by comparing version, definitions, methodology, and incentives.
9. State what is fact, inference, recommendation, and unknown.
10. Cite each important factual claim near the claim.

## Evidence Standard

- Never convert popularity, stars, downloads, or search rank into proof of quality.
- Do not use a review without inspecting what it actually evaluated.
- Do not claim a tool, API, benchmark, or feature exists without a source or direct observation.
- Treat source freshness separately from source authority.
- Prefer a small set of strong sources over a long undifferentiated bibliography.
- Say when evidence is insufficient and name the missing evidence.

Read [references/source-policy.md](references/source-policy.md) when planning a multi-source investigation or evaluating community skills.

## Output

Lead with the answer. Then provide:

- verified findings;
- important uncertainties or disagreements;
- recommendation with tradeoffs;
- sources attached to the claims they support.
